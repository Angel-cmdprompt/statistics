5. Interpretation

The results can be interpreted in terms of a collection of n systems exposed to m attackers.

In the Bernoulli model, each step contributes either zero or one to the score. The cumulative score therefore represents the number of positive contributions across the n steps. For a fixed probability p, increasing the number of systems increases the expected total score proportionally to n, while the average score becomes more concentrated around p.

In the Rademacher model, each step contributes either a positive or a negative unit. This model is useful for studying a balanced stochastic process in which positive and negative contributions have equal probability. The cumulative score fluctuates around zero, but the average score converges to zero as the number of steps increases. Its normalized total approaches a standard normal distribution.

The number of attackers m determines how many possible trajectories are observed in the simulation. A larger m provides a clearer picture of the distribution of possible outcomes. Nevertheless, the model does not establish that adding attackers directly increases or decreases the penetration score, because the influence of attackers is not explicitly incorporated into the increment distribution.

Law of Large Numbers (LLN)

For i.i.d. increments with finite mean μ, Sₙ/n converges in probability to μ. Thus, for a large number of steps, the average score becomes increasingly stable around its expected value.

Central Limit Theorem (CLT)

For Rademacher increments, μ = 0 and σ² = 1. Therefore Sₙ/√n converges in distribution to a standard normal random variable N(0,1). The normalized scores should look increasingly bell-shaped across many simulated paths.
